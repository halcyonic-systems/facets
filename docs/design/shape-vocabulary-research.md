# Shape Vocabulary Research — Precedent Brief

*Research for bert-lenses #81 (palette shape vocabulary). Precedent-gathering only — no code changed, nothing posted to GitHub. Feeds a later design session; pairs with #78 (image export) for a W30 closure week.*

## 1. Why this matters

bert-lenses' whole differentiator is **lens fidelity**: Klir, Bunge, and Mobus are not three skins on one shape set, they're three distinct ontological commitments that happen to share a kernel (`web/src/kernel/`). Shape is one of the cheapest channels for carrying that distinction — cheaper than color (which is already spent on flow-kind: matter/energy/message) and cheaper than position. Every tradition surveyed below uses shape to encode a *role* distinction, not a decoration. The brief below asks: which role distinctions does bert-lenses already have kernel facts for, and which of the precedent shape languages best carries each one without smuggling in a lens-specific commitment where the kernel is neutral.

## 2. What bert-lenses renders today (ground truth)

Audited `web/src/canvas/lenses/{common,klir,bunge,mobus}.tsx`.

**Shared node chrome** (`common.tsx` `NodeBody`): every node is a circle or a rounded-rect square, switched by a single `isSquare` boolean per lens. No third shape exists anywhere in the renderer.

| Lens | Component | Environment (source/sink) | Boundary marking | Process-primitive badge | Interface/port |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Klir** (`klir.tsx`) | circle, recessed opacity/stroke | circle (same as component — `isSquare={false}` always) | none (boundary = investigator's drawn distinction, handled by canvas backdrop, not the node) | none | `NullPortView` — no ports at all |
| **Bunge** (`bunge.tsx`) | circle | **square** (`isSquare={thing.role === "Environment"}`) | rim accent ring (`boundaryRim`) on components directly coupled to ℰ — a *marking*, not a drawn perimeter | none | `NullPortView` |
| **Mobus** (`mobus.tsx`) | circle, with process-primitive badge (small corner glyph) | **square**, and **open** (unfilled, `envOpen={true}` — "epistemically unknowable") | reified as a real membrane ring drawn by the stage backdrop | yes — `PRIMITIVE_BADGE` corner glyph (filled/outline/corner dot per `STYLE.badge.form`) | **yes** — `PortView`: a pill/rounded-rect notch breaking the membrane, with a direction glyph `▸` receives / `◂` exports / `⇄` hybrid |

So the seed vocabulary the team already committed to (confirmed in code, matching the task brief's description of "the hal projection model"):
- **circle = component**, **square = environment object** (source/sink), consistently Bunge→Mobus; Klir deliberately flattens this because Klir's boundary is investigator-drawn, not ontological.
- **open/unfilled square = env object under Mobus specifically** (epistemic unknowability of source/sink internals) — this is a *Mobus-only* refinement of the shared square, not a base-vocabulary fact. Bunge's square is filled.
- **interfaces are not a shape at all** — they're a small rounded-rect *notch* riding on the boundary ring, with a direction glyph doing the semantic work. This only exists under Mobus (`NullPortView` elsewhere).
- **rim accent (a stroke ring), not a shape**, marks Bunge boundary-components — a deliberate non-shape choice, because Bunge's boundary set is a marked subset of ordinary components, not a distinct kind of thing.
- Badge glyphs (Mobus process-primitive: combine/split/impede/propel/buffer, per Fig. 4.5's five atomic processes) are the only place a *third* visual vocabulary layer exists today, and it's a corner decoration, not the node shape itself.

**Where shape distinctions are missing**: nothing currently distinguishes a **stock/store** node from an ordinary transformation-process component — both are plain circles today; only the corner badge (if present) tells you it's a buffer. Nothing distinguishes a **component that owns interfaces** from one that doesn't, other than the presence of port notches on the boundary ring near it (indirect, not a node-level cue). No shape currently encodes **directionality** at the node level (only at the port glyph and edge-marker level).

## 3. Precedent table

| Tradition | Glyph set | Ontological distinction each shape carries | What bert-lenses should take |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mobus's own SL icon set** (Ch. 4, Figs. 4.5, 4.9–4.10, 4.13) | Rounded rectangles for interfaces "penetrating" the boundary (Fig. 4.9); ovals *or* boxes for subsystems, explicitly declared interchangeable ("either kind of closed shape can be used," Fig. 4.6 caption); open rectangles (unfilled) for environmental sources/sinks, colored by category (Fig. 4.7); orange triangle for a decision/regulator process (Fig. 4.17); five atomic-process icons (combine, split, impede, propel, buffer/stock) per Fig. 4.5, undescribed graphically in the text beyond "the various arrows... will be explained below" — the actual glyphs are only in the (unavailable) figure image, not recoverable from the prose. | Interface = **boundary-penetrating** shape (a shape that literally straddles the membrane line, not a free-floating node) — this is the single most load-bearing convention in the house tradition, and bert-lenses already reproduces it structurally via `PortView`'s placement on the ring, just not yet as a distinct interface *shape* separate from the pill notch. Env sources/sinks = open/unfilled rectangles, colored by category — bert-lenses already does unfilled for Mobus env objects; category-coloring is not yet used (color is spent on flow-kind, correctly, per KIND_COLOR). Decision/regulator = triangle, distinct from ordinary process. Stock/buffer = the fifth atomic-process primitive, sibling to combine/split/impede/propel — Mobus treats "is this a stock" as same ontological tier as "is this a splitter," which argues for the badge-glyph tier (as already built) rather than a fifth node shape. | **Confirms current instinct** (rect-notch interface, open square env) is house-faithful, not invented. **New idea**: a **decision/regulator triangle** is Mobus-canonical and bert-lenses has no equivalent — currently a decision node is just a circle with a badge. **Caution**: Mobus text explicitly treats circle/box as *interchangeable* for ordinary subsystems (Fig. 4.6 caption) — there is no Mobus warrant for using shape to distinguish subsystem *sub-kinds* beyond process-primitive, env-object, and interface. Anything more granular (e.g., "double circle for interface-bearing component") is a bert-lenses invention, not a Mobus citation — flag it as such in the design session, don't attribute it to the house tradition. |
| **Stella / Forrester system dynamics** | Stock = rectangle ("container," the noun). Flow = thick directed pipe with a valve/spigot glyph (the verb, an activity, not a thing). Converter/auxiliary = circle (computes a value, doesn't accumulate). Connector = thin arrow (information dependency, not substance). Cloud = an unbounded source/sink (explicitly "outside the model boundary, capacity unlimited"). | The four shapes map to a strict noun/verb/adjective/preposition grammar: rectangle=accumulates, pipe-with-valve=rate-of-change-actor, circle=derived-value, thin-arrow=mere-dependency, cloud=deliberately-unmodeled. This is the most successful shape language in systems modeling because each glyph is legible as a part of speech before the viewer reads a single label — the grammar does classification work the label would otherwise have to do. | The valve-on-pipe convention for flow is the strongest transferable idea: bert-lenses draws flow edges as plain colored strokes today (matter/energy/message via `KIND_COLOR` + width), with no glyph marking *rate-controllability* on the edge itself. Consider a valve/gate glyph at the *port* end of a flow (where a Mobus interface's protocol actually gates it) rather than mid-edge — this is closer to Mobus's own model (interfaces gate, edges don't) than Stella's is. The cloud-as-unbounded-source is essentially what Mobus's open square already does; no new shape needed there. |
| **Odum's energy systems language** | A full glyph alphabet keyed to ontological *function*, not just role: producer (a bullet/pointed shape that "collects and transforms low-quality energy"), consumer/storage tank ("Bertalanffy module," named for GST — a rounded tank shape, function = feedback autocatalysis), interaction (an X or pointed-intersection glyph = nonlinear coupling of two pathways), switch, sensor, transaction (money-loop), and more — each one visually distinct and tied to a specific systems-ecology role. | This is the strongest historical precedent for **"shape carries ontology" at scale** — Odum built an entire principled vocabulary (dozens of glyphs) where every shape is a load-bearing claim about what kind of thing a node *is*, not just where it sits. Notably he named the storage glyph after Bertalanffy specifically because it encodes GST's own concept of a store — a direct lineage to the Mobus 8-tuple's stock concept bert-lenses already formalizes in Lean. | Odum's scale (dozens of glyphs) is a **cautionary example, not a target** — it is famously hard to read without training, the opposite of bert-lenses' "faithful *and* legible" mandate (`project_lenses_stage_tool_over_realism`). The right lesson is selective: adopt Odum's principle (shape = ontological claim, never decoration) but keep bert-lenses' alphabet small (3–5 shapes total across all lenses), closer to Stella's economy than Odum's completeness. The storage/Bertalanffy-module naming is worth citing directly in the design doc as historical grounding for treating "stock" as its own primitive if the badge-glyph tier ever gets promoted to a node-shape tier. |
| **SysML block/port notation** | Block = rectangle with `«block»` keyword stereotype. Port = a small square **straddling the block's boundary edge**, typed, with an in/out flow direction shown by an arrow or fill convention (proxy port = shows only what's visible through it; full port = has its own features). | Ports are drawn exactly where they functionally sit — overlapping the boundary line of the owning block, not inside or outside it. This is the same geometric idea as Mobus Fig. 4.9's "interfaces penetrate the boundary," arrived at independently in software/systems engineering. Proxy vs. full port distinguishes "pass-through visibility" from "the port itself has behavior" — a distinction bert-lenses doesn't currently need (its interfaces are uniformly behavior-bearing, carrying a protocol object φ) but is worth naming as a considered-and-rejected option. | Directly validates the current `PortView` placement (pill notch on the ring, straddling the membrane) as industry-convergent, not idiosyncratic. SysML's **direction arrow on the port itself** (rather than a separate glyph) is a candidate simplification: bert-lenses currently uses a *text* glyph (▸/◂/⇄) inside the pill — an arrowhead notch shape (like SysML's) could replace or reinforce that, making direction legible even before the glyph renders (e.g. at small zoom). |
| **Automata (DFA/NFA) convention** | States = circles. Accepting states = **double circles** (concentric ring). Start state = an arrow from nowhere. Transitions = directed labeled edges. | Double-circle is a *state-tier* marking — it says "this state, same kind as all the others, is additionally distinguished by membership in a special subset (F ⊆ Q)." It is structurally identical to Bunge's boundary-rim-accent move (a marked subset of ordinary nodes) but rendered as concentric geometry instead of a color/stroke accent. | This directly tests Shingai's instinct ("component = double circle"). Given the automata precedent, double-circle is the right shape *grammar* for "this component is additionally a member of a marked subset" — i.e., it's the **same semantic slot Bunge's `boundaryRim` already fills**, just rendered differently. **Recommendation for the design session**: reserve double-circle specifically for the case that's actually FSA-analogous in bert-lenses — a component that is a **designated accepting/terminal state** in a Mobus-mode-as-DLG rendering (per the DLG-native finding in `project_bert_lenses_llm_integration.md`), not as a generic "has an interface" marker. Using double-circle for "has a port" would overload a well-known convention (accepting-state) with an unrelated meaning (interface-bearing), which risks the opposite of legibility — a viewer who knows automata will misread it. Interface-bearing is already carried by the port notch itself; it doesn't need a second, competing node-shape cue. |
| **Petri nets** (brief) | Places = circles (state/resource holder). Transitions = bars/rectangles (action that changes state). Tokens = filled dots inside places. Bipartite: arcs only ever connect place→transition or transition→place, never place→place. | The place/transition split is exactly Mobus's stock/process split (accumulator vs. transformer) and exactly Stella's rectangle/circle split — three independent traditions converge on **container-shape ≠ actor-shape**. The bipartite arc constraint (never same-kind-to-same-kind) parallels the kernel's bipartite `G` graph fact bert-lenses already renders specially for Mobus exo-flows (two-segment routing through the port). | Reinforces (doesn't add new information beyond Stella) that **stock/accumulator deserves a different shape from ordinary process**, if bert-lenses ever promotes the badge-glyph "buffer" primitive to full node-shape status. Token-as-dot is the same visual grammar bert-lenses already uses for the sim-fill clip-path animation (`frac`-based fill in `NodeBody`) — no new idea, just confirms the existing sim-readout affordance is genre-appropriate. |
| **Circuit notation** (brief, for completeness) | Component-specific glyphs (resistor zigzag, capacitor parallel bars, etc.) tied to a fixed, closed vocabulary of physical device types; wires are plain lines, direction shown by conventional current-flow arrows only where necessary. | Circuit notation is a *closed-world* vocabulary — every glyph names a specific, non-extensible physical primitive. Not directly transferable: bert-lenses is deliberately open-world (any domain-specific component name), so a circuit-style fixed glyph-per-thing-name approach would fight the tool's genericity. | No action item — cited only to explain why it's the wrong precedent to reach for, despite superficially being "a good shape language too." Systems modeling notations (Stella, Odum, Mobus, Petri) all stay generic-role-based; circuit notation is device-based. Keep bert-lenses in the generic-role family. |

## 4. Candidate shape mapping — neutral base + per-lens refinements

Framed per the lens-fidelity constraint: **shapes that encode a lens-specific commitment must appear only under that lens.** Klir/Bunge stay neutral; Mobus gets the richer vocabulary because Mobus is the lens that reifies boundary, interface, and process-primitive as first-class kernel facts.

### Neutral base (all three lenses, or Klir's deliberate flattening of it)

| Shape | Meaning | Kernel fact it renders | Lens scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Circle | ordinary system component | `thing.role === "Component"` | Bunge, Mobus (Klir flattens — see below) |
| Square | environment object (source/sink) | `thing.role === "Environment"` | Bunge (filled), Mobus (open/unfilled) |
| **Circle, uniformly, for everything** | Klir's deliberate refusal to shape-differentiate role | — | **Klir only** — this is not a gap to fill, it's the lens's own commitment: "thinghood taken for granted; the relation is salient" (comment in `klir.tsx`). Any shape-vocabulary work must NOT leak role-shapes into Klir. |

### Per-lens refinements

**Bunge** — adds only a *marking*, never a new shape: rim-accent ring for boundary-component membership (existing `boundaryRim`). No changes recommended; Bunge's restraint here is correct per the Bunge Def 1.2 semantics (boundary = a marked subset, not a distinct kind).

**Mobus** — three live candidates, ranked:

1. **(Recommended, low-risk)** Keep interfaces as boundary-straddling notches (current `PortView`), but replace the current plain rounded-rect with a shape that echoes SysML's port-square-on-the-edge more explicitly, and consider folding the direction glyph into the notch's *geometry* (an arrowhead-shaped notch for Receives/Exports, a diamond or double-notch for Hybrid) rather than relying solely on the ▸/◂/⇄ text glyph — this reads before the glyph renders, and degrades gracefully at small zoom (ties into #78 image export, where labels may be too small to read but shape silhouettes survive).
2. **(Recommended, low-risk)** Add a **triangle** for decision/regulator processes, matching Mobus Fig. 4.17's orange-triangle convention directly. This is currently invisible (a circle + badge only) despite being explicitly canonical in the house text. Scope strictly to Mobus (decision/regulation is a Mobus process-primitive fact, not a Klir/Bunge one).
3. **(Contested — needs the design session, not this brief)** Whether "component that owns one or more interfaces" deserves its own node shape (e.g., double-circle) distinct from an interface-less component. Per the automata-precedent analysis above, this specific proposal is **not recommended** — double-circle already has a strong, different meaning (accepting/terminal state) that the DLG-native work (`project_bert_lenses_llm_integration.md`) may eventually want to use faithfully for actual FSA semantics. Reusing it for "has a port" pre-empts that and risks misreading by anyone automata-literate. If interface-ownership needs a node-level (not just boundary-level) cue at all, prefer a lighter mark — e.g., a small tick or notch on the node's own rim at the angle facing its port, echoing SysML's port-overlapping-the-block-edge convention — rather than a competing closed shape.

### Two-to-three-option table for the contested point (#3 above), for the design session to pick from

| Option | Shape | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| A | Double circle = interface-bearing component | Collides with automata "accepting state" semantics; blocks future FSA-faithful use |
| B | Double circle reserved for actual DLG/FSA accepting-state rendering (future); interface-bearing gets a rim tick instead | Two visual grammars to maintain, but each stays semantically clean |
| C | No node-level cue at all; interface-bearing is legible only via the port notches already on the boundary ring near it | Cheapest, most restrained; matches Bunge's "mark, don't invent a shape" discipline; may be too subtle at low zoom / in exported images |

No recommendation is made between B and C here — that's exactly the kind of call the design session should make with the actual canvas in front of it, weighing #78 image-export legibility against vocabulary economy.

## 5. Open design questions (crisp, closeable)

1. **Double-circle disposition**: adopt Option B, C, or A (not recommended) from §4's table above — settles whether "interface-bearing" ever gets a competing node shape, and reserves or releases double-circle for future FSA/DLG accepting-state work.
2. **Decision/regulator triangle**: add it to Mobus as a fourth node shape (alongside circle/square/port-notch), scoped strictly to the `Decide` process-primitive badge case — yes/no, and if yes, does it replace or supplement the existing corner badge glyph for that primitive?
3. **Port notch geometry**: keep the current rounded-rect-with-text-glyph, or move direction semantics into the notch's silhouette (arrowhead/diamond) for zoom- and export-robustness ahead of #78?
4. **Stock/buffer shape promotion**: does the fifth Mobus atomic-process primitive (buffer/stock, Fig. 4.5) ever get promoted from badge-glyph to full node shape (Stella/Petri-net precedent argues yes, at some scale of model complexity), or does the sim-fill fraction visualization (already built in `NodeBody`) already do that job well enough that a shape change is unnecessary? Recommend deferring — no strong precedent forces this now, and the existing fill-clip animation already gives stocks a visually distinct *behavior* even with a shared shape.
5. **Env-object category coloring**: Mobus Fig. 4.7 colors sources/sinks by category (energy source, material source, product sink, waste sink, etc.) using open rectangles; bert-lenses currently uses `KIND_COLOR` for flow *edges* only, leaving env-object squares uncolored/neutral. Should env-object fill or stroke ever pick up `KIND_COLOR` from its dominant connected flow kind, or does that fight the existing "environment = epistemically unknowable, hence unfilled/neutral" framing? This is a color question riding on the shape research, worth a one-line disposition even though it's out of scope for the shape vocabulary itself.
